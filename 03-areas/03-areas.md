# Areas

**Purpose**: subjects and responsibilities that never end (wizard step 5 in [ORGANIZE.md](../99-meta/ORGANIZE.md)). In INPARA an area **is** a map of content: one note, `03-areas/<area>.md`, with no folder.

## What an area map holds

- A short purpose and scope.
- Links to the notes, resources and projects the area uses, each with a reason, grouped by subtopic.
- Patterns noticed across those links, open questions, and potential projects.

Everything it links lives elsewhere: our notes in `01-notes/`, captured material in `04-resources/`, work in `02-projects/`. A living page about the area, such as a catalogue or a status page, is a handbook note in `01-notes/01h-handbook/` that the map links. Start from [area-map.md](../99-meta/templates/area-map.md). Each area is one row in [tags.md](../99-meta/tags.md), added in the same change as its map, and every note with that area in its `area` front matter is linked from the map.

## When to make an area

Make one when you notice:
- **5 or more related notes** on one subject
- **frequent links** between the same notes
- **an ongoing responsibility** that needs steady attention
- **a domain** you keep working in

## Areas and maps

Areas and projects are the human front end: a person browses maps. Notes and resources are kept by agents, and every one of them is reachable from some map. An item can belong to many areas: each map links it, and nothing is copied.

## Tips

- **Start with obvious areas.** Don't force artificial boundaries.
- **Keep the number manageable.** 5 to 8 areas is plenty for most people.
- **Split a map** when its scope grows too broad; link the two maps to each other.
- **Too narrow?** It is probably a project. **Has an end?** It is a project.
